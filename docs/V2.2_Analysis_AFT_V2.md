# T9Sim v2.2 — what the two data layers are worth

## Results and analysis

Having designed the T9 BN, generated 10 datasets of 10 million rows each, censored them into 4
views, trained them into 5 ML predictors and assembled one bidding algorithm for each view, can we
now answer the question how much incremental value each of the MMP and SSP data layer holds for a
DSP bidding algorithm?

Again, C1 is the DSP view. C2 is the plus MMP view. C3 is the plus SSP view. C4 is the all 3
layers view.

The metrics in a few words: **spend CRPS** is the error of the whole predicted spend distribution,
in dollars. **EV level** is what the bidder thinks the inventory is worth over what it is really
worth. **rmse_log** is the size of the price head's error, in log dollars. **bias_log** is the
direction of that error, zero being unbiased. **bid rate** and **win rate / auctions** are shares
of every auction in the test set; **win rate / bids** is the share of the auctions the bidder
actually entered. A bidder that bids on 20 per cent and wins 5 has won 25% of the auctions it
entered.

## RQ1. How much is the MMP data layer worth to a DSP's bidding algorithm?

Both tier 1 value heads and bidder performance all improved significantly when lost auction's
outcomes are revealed to the DSP. Profit jumped by 221%, EV (expected value) estimate moved to
0.897 against the oracle ceiling of 1. The harder predictions improved more; payers v clicks
+0.0948 v 0.022. The bidder is also encouraged to bid more.

### Prediction performance

All four funnel heads improve, and one of them is not unanimous. Click, install and payer move
with every seed agreeing and every interval clear of zero. Spend does not, because the dataset is
not large enough to resolve it. Across the ten seeds C1's spend head is graded on 75 payers on
average, range 29 to 103, against C2's mean 624.

The gain deepens down the funnel. Each stage depends on the one before it clearing, so the
survivors grow scarcer at every step and harder to identify. This is where the C2 MMP data for
install, payer and spend data helps most. Click +0.0220, install +0.0377, payer +0.0948.

Tier 2 does not move, as expected. MMP data reveals user conversion outcomes on the rows C1 lost.
It reveals nothing about prices, so it could not have moved the price head.

### Economic value

The valuation is transformed, and it buys volume as well as value. EV level rises from 0.532 to
0.897, meaning that C1 believes the inventory is worth about half what it is, C2 nearly all of it,
and profit rises 221% with every seed agreeing.

C2 bids more. C2 bids on 27.9% of auctions against C1's 21.7%. The ROAS gate `ev/b* ≥ 3` decides
whether to bid, and a higher valuation makes the bidder bid more. EV level rising from 0.532 in C1
to 0.897 in C2 is the reason why C2 bids more.

C2 wins more. EV also sets how much to bid, through `argmax_b (ev − b)·P(win | b)`. A larger EV
widens the surplus term, so buying win probability becomes worthwhile and the argmax climbs the
ladder. Higher bids in turn raise the win rate over bids, from C1's 25.6% to C2's 32.6%.

### Prediction performance

`rmse_log` falls by 0.4017, `bias_log` disappears and win AUC improves, agreed with all 10 seeds.
This shows clear SSP data lift on prediction performance.

Tier 1 heads do not move, as expected. SSP data provides auction price information but not user
values, so tier 1 heads do not move.

### Economic value

C1 and C3 value inventory identically, at an EV level of 0.532 against 0.534, so nothing here comes
from knowing users better. Everything comes from the price head, and it reaches the bidder through
`argmax_b (ev − b)·P(win | b)`. C1's head over-predicts the clearing price by a factor of 1.58,
which shifts its win curve to the right: it believes any given bid is less likely to win than it is.

That misallocates its bidding in both directions at once. C1 bids **3.20 CPM on average against
C3's 2.86**, every seed agreeing, yet only **1.69 on the auctions it actually enters against C3's
1.88**, also unanimous. It spends too much chasing rows it cannot win and too little on the ones it
can.

Both effects help C3. Bidding lower overall raises `ev/b*`, so more rows clear the gate
`ev/b* ≥ 3` and the bid rate rises from 21.7 to 23.8 per cent. Bidding higher where it competes
puts the bid nearer the true clearing price, so the win rate over bids rises from **25.6 to 30.3
per cent**, a gain of 3.4 points with every seed agreeing. The same 3.4 points appear on top of
MMP, so a pricing improvement raises the share of bids that clear by the same amount whether or not
the valuation layer is present.

Profit per win falls by 2.85 and volume more than repays it.

### Both layers together, C4 − C1

**The integrated stack earns 3.7 times what a DSP-only bidder earns**, every seed agreeing. That is
the figure the study exists to produce, and it is what an operator who owns all three layers holds
over one who owns one.

**The two layers compound almost exactly, and neither gets in the other's way.** Measurement alone
multiplies profit by 3.211 and supply data alone by 1.154. If the two were independent their
product would be 3.678; the measured figure is **3.748**, a ratio of **1.017 [0.998, 1.035]**. The
interval touches 1, so the small excess is not established, but the absence of interference is: two
layers bought separately deliver what they promise when bought together.

**They arrive by different routes, which is why they do not collide.** Measurement moves the
valuation and never touches the price head. Supply data moves the price head and never touches the
valuation. The bidder reads both through the same two rules, so their effects meet only at the bid
and multiply rather than overlap.

**The stack buys volume above all.** Bid rate rises from 21.7 to 30.5 per cent and the win rate over
bids from 25.6 to 36.2, so C4 enters 41 per cent more auctions and converts a much larger share of
them. It spends 3.3 times as much to earn 3.2 times as much profit, which is the shape of a bidder
that has stopped guessing rather than one that has learned to haggle.
